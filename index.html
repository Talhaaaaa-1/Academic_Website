Research statement

Most data-centre twins assume the coolant never boils.

In the data-centre twins I have studied, ExaDigiT and DCVerse among them, the cold plate is treated as a fixed thermal resistance and the coolant stays single-phase. For a single-phase loop this is a reasonable simplification.

Two-phase cooling is a different case. Boiling inside the cold plate, or on the chip itself, carries most of the heat, and one fixed resistance cannot describe it. Most of my research sits in this gap. With Prof. Ibraheem Haneef, I am writing a critical review of how ready physics-informed digital twins are for direct-to-chip and immersion cooling.

The practical question

How much boiling and conjugate heat transfer physics does a twin need before its temperature predictions are fit for real-time control?

Chip

Current twins handle this case well enough.

Here the model has to resolve boiling and conjugate heat transfer in some form, and the right level of detail is not yet settled.

Physics-informed digital twins for direct-to-chip and immersion cooling

The review asks how close physics-informed digital twins are to real-time use in data centres cooled by direct-to-chip and immersion systems. Much of it comes down to the gap described above: where current twins simplify the cold plate and the coolant, and what a twin would need to model boiling in its place. In parallel, I am building twins of heat transfer and cooling performance for both cooling routes.

My first draft drew on a corpus of 48 earlier reviews and surveys from five databases (2014 to 2026) and went to Prof. Haneef in September 2026. For the second draft, now in progress, the working corpus has grown to 184 papers in 12 topic groups, and I have compared 11 earlier reviews across 12 dimensions to place this one among them. The review also sets out two grading scales: a DT0 to DT6 ladder for twin maturity, and V0 to V5 for how far a twin has been validated against measured data.

Tandem flexible flags behind a bluff body at Re = 300

In our group's earlier water-tunnel study, Mujtaba et al. (2021) found that two piezoelectric flags in tandem behind an inverted C-shaped cylinder harvested 116% more power than a single flag. They reported the rear flag's peak power at a streamwise gap of 1.75. This 2D study at Re = 300 looks at the same arrangement numerically, on the group's in-house immersed-boundary FSI solver, written in Fortran, in which the flags move through a fixed grid. The body-to-flag and flag-to-flag gaps each take six values from 0.25 to 1.5, which gives 36 cases.

My part is running the cases and the Python post-processing. The scripts trim the start-up transient, check each run for solver divergence or early termination, and batch the Tecplot figures of drag and lift on both flags, bending and kinetic energy, tail motion, flag shape and vorticity. Some runs are now being corrected so that all 36 can be used. Contour maps across the gap grid come next, and then a journal paper.

Final-year thesis · Jun 2023 – May 2024

Multi-axis self-stabilising platform for heavy payloads

Our team of four designed a two-axis gimbal, pitch and yaw, that holds a heavy payload in place against outside disturbance. The motors, frame and mounts were modelled in SOLIDWORKS. We chose aluminium for the frame, which was cast and then checked for stress, deformation and fatigue in ANSYS. Actuators were sized from the inertia ratios, torque and RPM each axis needed.

The control system was my part (Mar – Aug 2023). I wrote the Arduino software that drives the BLDC servos by PWM and reads the angles from an MPU6050 IMU, tuned the PID loops, and added fail-safes that hold the payload through vibration or sudden shifts. A teammate wrote the Kalman filter that cleans up the sensor noise. What made it hard was scale. Each motor was rated at 1000 W and the drivers drew up to 40 A, so wiring and testing needed far more care than a bench project. I also bought the components from China and coordinated building the prototype. It was tested for step response and robustness to disturbance, and we recommended a third axis with better controllers as the next step.

Mechanics of Materials course project · Sep 2022 – Jan 2023

Lunar Electric Rover: structural design without FEA

The brief asked for a lunar electric rover whose structure was checked by hand, with no finite element analysis allowed. Our team of five arrived at a design of about 3,700 kg: six suspension legs with two wheels each, a two-level I-beam chassis, and a round-topped cabin for two people. Nothing was built; the parts were modelled in SOLIDWORKS and mated into a full assembly.

I scripted the hand calculations in MATLAB, from the statically indeterminate chassis frame to column buckling, the pressurised cabin and wheel-shaft fatigue. Temperature mattered more than gravity. The lunar day and night swing, about 120 °C to −130 °C, puts roughly 200 to 220 MPa of thermal stress into restrained members. Once a 1 m drop and buckling were added to the checks, aluminium 6061 fell short, so the final design moved to aluminium 7068 with a Ti-6Al-4V suspension.

Feb – Jun 2023 · Carrier HAP

Cooling-load estimation for a two-storey house

HVAC course project, team of five. I estimated the cooling load of each conditioned room in a house placed in Lahore for the weather data, first in Carrier's Hourly Analysis Program (HAP) and then by hand: envelope conduction, solar gain through glazing, occupants, lighting and equipment. In five of the six rooms worked both ways, the hand figure came out 9–12% above HAP. The kitchen, where the stove dominates, went the other way: 19% below (22,362 against 27,600 Btu/hr). The report attributes the gaps to infiltration, which HAP includes and the hand method left out, and to the assumed solar intensity.

Feb – Jun 2021 · Python, NumPy, Matplotlib

Interactive stress-strain model

A bilinear stress-strain curve up to 5% strain: linear elastic to the yield point, then linear hardening with a slope of 5% of the modulus. Temperature lowers the modulus and yield strength by 0.05% per °C above 25 °C, down to a floor of 30%, and each °C/s of cooling rate above 1 °C/s adds 2% to the yield strength. Both factors are simple assumptions rather than fits to test data. Four sliders set Young's modulus, yield strength, cooling rate and temperature, and the curve is redrawn as they move.
